# Premium Product Advertising Design Architect

**Reusable skill for generating high-quality commercial graphics in one
pass**

## Mission

Turn a product, brand, offer, and target audience into a polished,
conversion-focused advertising graphic. Use the uploaded reference as a
guide to its *design architecture*, not as something to copy literally.

The visual language is premium commercial product advertising:
controlled studio art direction, strong hierarchy, restrained colors,
realistic product staging, clean typography, and clear purchase
information.

## 1. Core design principles

1.  **One clear visual priority.** The product is the hero; the offer is
    the commercial anchor; the brand is immediately recognizable.
2.  **Hierarchy before decoration.** The viewer should understand, in
    order: brand → product → offer/price → key benefits → product
    proof/details → call to action or service footer.
3.  **Premium restraint.** Use a limited palette, deliberate negative
    space, crisp alignment, and only the visual effects that improve
    clarity.
4.  **Product fidelity is mandatory.** Preserve the supplied product's
    actual silhouette, proportions, components, finish, color, labels,
    logo placement, and distinctive details. Never invent features or
    redesign the product.
5.  **Realistic staging.** Use believable perspective, contact shadows,
    reflections, material response, and coherent lighting. Avoid
    floating products, impossible geometry, or decorative clutter.
6.  **Commercial clarity.** Price, model, key benefit, availability, and
    CTA must be easy to locate and read.
7.  **Language integrity.** Reproduce supplied wording accurately. For
    Arabic or Kurdish Sorani, use correct right-to-left layout and
    proper glyph shaping. Do not fabricate pseudo-text or silently
    change meaning.
8.  **Reference-led, not reference-locked.** Transfer the layout logic
    and quality bar, while adapting colors, environment, typography, and
    composition to the actual brand and product category.

## 2. Default design architecture

Adapt the layout to the format and product; do not force every section
into every design.

### A. Brand zone

-   Place the supplied logo near the top, with clear space around it.
-   Preserve the logo's exact proportions and colors unless explicitly
    asked to recolor it.
-   Do not create a competing decorative logo or watermark.

### B. Product-title zone

-   Use a large, bold, short headline or model name.
-   Establish a clear contrast between headline and supporting copy.
-   Keep line breaks intentional; avoid cramped lettering and awkward
    wrapping.

### C. Offer anchor

-   Put the price, discount, or main offer in a high-contrast badge or
    compact block.
-   Make the currency and number accurate and unambiguous.
-   Never invent a price, discount, guarantee, specification, or claim.
-   If no offer is provided, use a concise value proposition or omit
    this zone.

### D. Benefits row

-   Use two to four short, relevant benefits.
-   Pair each benefit with a simple, consistent line icon when icons
    improve comprehension.
-   Keep labels concise and readable; do not use icons as decoration
    alone.
-   Use only claims supplied by the user or established in the product
    brief.

### E. Hero product scene

-   Allocate the largest visual area to the product.
-   Use a believable premium environment: architectural surfaces, studio
    plinths, clean workspaces, subtle material textures, or a
    category-appropriate setting.
-   For multiple units, create depth with a dominant foreground unit and
    smaller supporting units. Do not duplicate products unless
    requested.
-   Use controlled key lighting, realistic fill, edge separation,
    natural contact shadows, and reflections appropriate to the
    materials.
-   Maintain a coherent camera angle and physically plausible
    perspective.
-   Keep the background quiet enough that the product remains
    unmistakable.

### F. Service / CTA footer

-   Use a restrained bottom strip for delivery, installation, warranty,
    availability, contact, or a call to action, if supplied.
-   Separate footer content with spacing or a fine divider rather than
    heavy boxes.
-   Keep icons, text, and separators aligned.
-   Do not add unsupported service promises.

## 3. Visual system

### Palette

Start with a neutral base---off-white, charcoal, black, stone gray, or a
category-appropriate background---and one primary brand accent. A second
accent is optional. Use the accent strategically for the offer, one
headline detail, or a small set of emphasis points.

Do not blindly reuse the reference's red/black/white palette. Derive the
palette from the supplied logo, product, and campaign objective.

### Typography

-   Use one strong display typeface and one highly readable supporting
    typeface, or a disciplined family with different weights.
-   Use clear scale contrast: headline, offer, benefit labels, footer.
-   Prefer bold geometric or neo-grotesque sans-serif typography for
    contemporary product ads.
-   Avoid too many font families, excessive letter spacing, fake
    condensed fonts, and text effects that harm legibility.
-   Ensure small text remains readable at the final output size.

### Composition

-   Use a clear grid and consistent alignment.
-   Preserve deliberate negative space around the logo, headline, price,
    and product silhouette.
-   Create depth through foreground/background separation, scale, and
    lighting---not through random decorative shapes.
-   Balance visual mass; do not leave an accidental dead zone or crowd
    all copy into one corner.
-   Make the composition work at the requested aspect ratio, not just as
    a cropped version of another format.

### Finish

Aim for clean edges, controlled contrast, realistic materials, premium
lighting, precise spacing, and a finished commercial-art-direction look.
Avoid generic AI gloss, excessive bloom, oversharpening, fake text,
malformed icons, warped logos, impossible product parts, and needless
lens flares.

## 4. One-shot workflow

Before generating, internally resolve the following from the brief: 1.
What is being sold, and what must remain visually exact? 2. Who is the
audience, and what is the single main message? 3. What is the most
important commercial detail: price, benefit, model, launch, or CTA? 4.
What is the output format, aspect ratio, and placement (feed, story,
banner, print, storefront)? 5. Which supplied assets are mandatory:
product photo, logo, copy, brand colors, icons, contact details? 6.
Which claims are confirmed, and which information is missing? 7. What
composition best serves the product category? 8. Is all text short
enough to remain legible and accurate?

Then produce the finished design directly. Do not ask questions if the
brief already contains enough information; make sensible design
decisions for nonessential gaps. If a missing fact is
critical---especially price, contact details, or mandatory wording---do
not fabricate it. Omit it or ask one concise question.

## 5. Master prompt template

Copy this prompt whenever you want to generate a new design:

> **Act as a senior art director and premium commercial advertising
> designer.**
>
> Create a finished, professional advertising graphic for **\[PRODUCT /
> MODEL\]** for **\[BRAND\]**, targeting **\[AUDIENCE\]**, with the goal
> of **\[SALES / AWARENESS / LAUNCH / LEADS\]**.
>
> **Output:** \[ASPECT RATIO and pixel dimensions\], for \[PLATFORM /
> PLACEMENT\].
>
> **Use these assets faithfully:** \[PRODUCT REFERENCE\], \[LOGO\],
> \[OTHER ASSETS\].
>
> **Exact copy that must appear:**\
> Brand: \[TEXT\]\
> Headline/model: \[TEXT\]\
> Price/offer: \[TEXT or NONE\]\
> Benefits: \[TEXT\]\
> CTA/contact/footer: \[TEXT or NONE\]\
> Language(s): \[LANGUAGE(S)\]
>
> **Art direction:** Use premium product-advertising design
> architecture: a clear brand zone, bold product headline, high-contrast
> offer anchor when relevant, concise benefit row, dominant realistic
> product hero scene, and a restrained service/CTA footer when relevant.
> Adapt the order and proportions to the format rather than forcing a
> rigid template.
>
> **Visual style:** \[BRAND COLORS / MOOD / ENVIRONMENT\]. Use
> controlled studio lighting, physically plausible perspective,
> realistic contact shadows and reflections, premium materials,
> deliberate negative space, precise grid alignment, and restrained
> visual effects. Make the product the main visual priority.
>
> **Non-negotiable fidelity:** Preserve the real product's shape,
> proportions, components, finish, labels, and identifying details.
> Preserve the supplied logo without distortion. Do not invent product
> specifications, prices, discounts, ratings, certifications,
> guarantees, availability, or service claims. Reproduce the required
> text accurately.
>
> **Typography and localization:** Use a clear typographic hierarchy and
> readable text. For Arabic or Kurdish Sorani, use proper right-to-left
> layout, correct spelling and glyph shaping, and culturally appropriate
> spacing. Do not mirror product imagery or logos just to accommodate
> RTL text.
>
> **Quality control:** Check hierarchy, alignment, contrast, margins,
> product fidelity, text spelling, currency, icon consistency,
> perspective, lighting, and legibility at the target size. Remove
> clutter and any element that does not help explain the product or sell
> the offer.
>
> Deliver one cohesive, production-minded final composition. Do not
> provide a mood board, multiple unrelated concepts, or explanatory text
> in place of the design.

## 6. Optional style modifiers

Append only the modifiers that fit the brief:

-   **Luxury:** more negative space, fewer words, subtle materials,
    restrained highlights, precise typography.
-   **Industrial / hardware:** tactile surfaces, engineered precision,
    realistic metal finishes, strong geometry, practical benefits.
-   **Consumer electronics:** clean studio scene, refined reflections,
    controlled gradients, crisp silhouette, minimal UI-like details.
-   **Furniture / interiors:** believable room scale, natural material
    relationships, warm or directional light, product integrated into
    the space.
-   **High-impact retail:** stronger price contrast, compact offer
    messaging, direct CTA, clear benefit hierarchy.
-   **Social story / reel cover:** one dominant message, large product,
    safe margins, mobile-first text size, simple footer.
-   **Arabic / Kurdish localization:** correct RTL reading order,
    locally appropriate currency formatting, native-language review, no
    fake calligraphy or pseudo-text.

## 7. Final quality gate

Before accepting the result, verify:

-   [ ] The product is recognizable and faithful to the reference.
-   [ ] The logo is intact, correctly proportioned, and not recreated
    inaccurately.
-   [ ] The viewer can identify the product and main message within two
    seconds.
-   [ ] The price and currency, if present, are exact.
-   [ ] All required copy is present and spelled correctly.
-   [ ] The hierarchy is obvious and spacing is consistent.
-   [ ] The product has believable lighting, perspective, shadows, and
    material response.
-   [ ] The accent color is controlled rather than scattered everywhere.
-   [ ] Footer details are readable and do not compete with the hero.
-   [ ] The composition fits the requested aspect ratio without awkward
    cropping.
-   [ ] No unsupported claims, random extra products, fake text, or
    decorative clutter remain.

## Important practical note

Image-generation models can still misspell text, distort logos, or
subtly alter product details. For exact commercial delivery, inspect the
generated result and correct critical typography or logo placement in a
design editor. The prompt reduces these errors; it cannot guarantee
perfect text rendering in every generation.
